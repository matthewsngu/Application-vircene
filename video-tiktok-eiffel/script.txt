You think you can't sell something you don't own. Wrong.
In 1925, a con man sold the Eiffel Tower. Then he tried to sell it again.
His name was Victor Lustig.
Paris, 1925. Lustig reads a newspaper story: the Eiffel Tower is rusting and costs a fortune to repair. He gets an idea.
He needs three things: fake government letterhead, a luxury hotel suite, and a good story.
So how did he pull it off?
He invites a group of scrap metal dealers to a secret meeting at the Hôtel de Crillon. He tells them the city will tear the tower down, in total secrecy. Seven thousand three hundred tons of iron. One dealer, André Poisson, wants it badly. When Poisson hesitates, Lustig hints at a bribe. Only a real official would ask for that. Poisson pays.
Lustig flees to Vienna with the money. Poisson is too ashamed to call the police.
And here's the twist: a month later, Lustig comes back and tries to sell the tower again.
He died in prison. His death certificate listed his job as apprentice salesman.
So be honest: would you have bought it?
