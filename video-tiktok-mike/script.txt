You think a chicken can't live without its head. Wrong.
In 1945, a headless chicken lived for eighteen months, and made his owners rich.
His name was Mike.
Fruita, Colorado, September 1945. Farmer Lloyd Olsen swings his axe for dinner. But Mike gets up, and keeps walking.
The axe missed three things: the jugular vein, one ear, and most of his brain stem.
So how does a chicken live without a head?
In a chicken, the brain stem runs breathing, heartbeat and walking. Lloyd fed him with an eyedropper: milk, water and tiny grains, straight down his throat. And Mike kept growing, from two and a half pounds to almost eight.
The Olsens took him on tour. People paid twenty-five cents to see him. Time and Life magazines wrote about him. The counter climbs: four thousand five hundred dollars a month.
And here's the twist: one night in a motel, Mike started choking. The syringe that cleared his throat had been left at the last show.
Today, his town still throws Mike a festival every year.
So be honest: would you have paid to see him?
