You think a war ends when the world says it's over. Wrong.
One soldier kept fighting World War Two for twenty-nine years after it ended.
His name was Hiroo Onoda.
Lubang Island, the Philippines, December 1944. Onoda is ordered to hold the island and never surrender.
In the jungle, he had three things: a rifle, a uniform, and his orders.
So how do you miss the end of a war?
In 1945, planes drop leaflets: the war is over. Onoda decides they are fake. More leaflets come, then newspapers, even letters from his family. He thinks it is all an enemy trick. His men surrender or die, one by one. His raids also kill local villagers. The counter climbs: over ten thousand days in the jungle.
1974. A young traveler named Norio Suzuki goes looking for him, and finds him. But Onoda will not leave without an order from his commander.
And here's the twist.
His old commander now sells books. He flies to the jungle, reads the order out loud, and the war finally ends.
