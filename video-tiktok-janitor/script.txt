You think you need a big salary to become a millionaire. Wrong.
A janitor left behind almost eight million dollars. Nobody had a clue.
His name was Ronald Read.
Born in 1921, near Brattleboro, Vermont. He fixed cars at a gas station for twenty-five years, then spent seventeen years cleaning a department store.
His whole life came down to three things: a small used car, an old coat held shut with safety pins, and solid stocks.
So how does it work?
His stocks paid him dividends. Ronald never spent them. He reinvested them. At seven percent a year, money doubles about every ten years. Over sixty years, every dollar gets multiplied by sixty-four. Even when Lehman Brothers collapsed in 2008, he kept going. The counter climbs: eight million.
June 2014. Ronald dies at ninety-two. In his safe deposit box: five inches of stock certificates. Even his family is stunned.
And here's the twist: he never had a big salary. Just time. And he leaves six million dollars to his town's hospital and library.
So if you started today, how much would you put aside?
