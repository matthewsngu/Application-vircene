You think a story must be true if Hollywood made a movie about it. Wrong.
A teenager claimed he faked being a pilot, a doctor and a lawyer, and cashed millions in fake checks.
His name is Frank Abagnale.
New York, the 1960s. Frank says he ran away from home at sixteen with almost nothing but confidence.
His legend had three parts: a Pan Am pilot uniform, a doctor's white coat, and a law degree he never earned.
So how did the story work?
He said he flew a million miles for free. He said he ran a hospital ward in Georgia and passed the bar exam in Louisiana. The counter climbs: two and a half million dollars in fake checks. He wrote a book, and Steven Spielberg turned it into a movie with Leonardo DiCaprio.
Frank did commit real fraud, and he did serve real time. But in 2020, a researcher checked the records. During much of his famous con career, Frank was sitting in prison.
And here's the twist: the greatest impostor's biggest con may have been his own life story.
So how many legends you believe are just a role?
